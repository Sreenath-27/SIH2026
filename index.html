AI-powered agricultural intelligence, from data to decisions

The platform monitors crops with Sentinel-2 imagery, combines it with weather data and historical field observations, and computes NDVI to track vegetation health over time. When it detects stress or anomalies, it sends recommendations to the farmer.

The problem it addresses

  • Monitors crops from Sentinel-2 satellite imagery.
  • Detects crop stress and abnormal conditions early through NDVI analysis.
  • Reduces dependence on manual field inspections.
  • Supports efficient irrigation and resource management.
  • Delivers timely alerts and recommendations.
  • Removes the need for expensive field sensors and hardware.

What makes it different

Most crop monitoring needs equipment in the field. Nexus works from orbit, so any farm the satellite can see can be monitored.

Technical approach in six stages

From raw satellite pixels to an alert on the farmer's phone.

1

Data acquisition

Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery, ISRO Bhoonidhi geospatial data, weather data (rainfall, temperature, humidity) and farmer input: location, crop type and field area.

2

Data preprocessing

Cloud removal, extracting the Red and NIR bands, georeferencing, resampling and masking, then preparing time-series data.

3

NDVI computation

Turns the Red and NIR bands into an NDVI map, shown next to the true-colour image on a scale from -1 to +1.

4

AI-based analysis

Crop health assessment (healthy, moderate or stressed), water stress detection for water-deficient areas, and anomaly detection for unusual patterns, disease risk and yield-loss potential.

5

Decision support

Generates insights and recommendations: irrigation suggestions, area-wise risk alerts, crop management advice and historical trend analysis.

6

Farmer output

Alerts and recommendations on a mobile or web dashboard, with easy-to-understand insights and actionable suggestions.

NDVI = (NIR − Red) / (NIR + Red)
Healthy leaves reflect near-infrared light and absorb red.

That contrast is what the satellite sees, so a falling NDVI over several dates is an early sign of stress.

Feasibility and viability

The data is free, the method is proven and the processing runs in the cloud. Known challenges each have a plan.

Challenges
  • Cloud cover affecting satellite imagery
  • Gaps between satellite observations
  • High-volume data processing requirements
  • Crop variability across regions
  • Weather-related interpretation
How we handle them
  • Historical and multi-date satellite analysis
  • Cloud-based processing using Google Earth Engine
  • Crop-specific AI models
  • Weather-data integration and continuous AI model refinement

Why it can be built now

  • Free, accessible satellite data on Google Earth Engine and Bhoonidhi.
  • NDVI-based crop monitoring is a proven approach.
  • Existing AI/ML technologies support implementation.
  • Cloud-based, large-scale monitoring with no hardware required.
